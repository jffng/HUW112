**Exercise**

Spend 10 minutes reflecting on your animation.

- *Hierarchy*
- *Rhythm & Pacing*
- *Restraint*
- *Intent*

1. **One thing that's working.** Name it specifically, and which principle it demonstrates. ('The title arriving first establishes hierarchy.')
2. **One thing you would change.** Name what it is, why you'd change it, and which principle is doing the work. ('The subtitle and background fade in at exactly the same time, which competes for attention. I'd offset them so the subtitle arrives slightly later — that's a hierarchy issue.')
3. **One thing you're not sure about.** A question you have. ('I don't know if the title takes too long to arrive — does the timing feel right?')
4. **Sound: yes, no, or maybe?** Is your animation a piece that wants sound, or wants silence? If yes, what kind of sound would serve it?

The three items don't have to be perfect. The point is to look closely enough to have something specific to say.

After 10 minutes. 7 minutes per person, then switch. 

When it's your turn, here's the order:

1. **Show your animation once, silently.** Don't pre-explain or apologize.
2. **Listen to your partner's response.** They get one minute to say what they noticed. Where did their eye go? What stood out? 
3. **Then read them what you wrote.** Your three items — working, would change, uncertain. They listen.
4. **Discuss the uncertain.** Use the rest of your time on the question you weren't sure about.

**Requirements**
- Turn in two video versions of your animated poster:
    - **Version 1 (draft):** your first animation, before revision
    - **Version 2 (revised):** your animation after revision, informed by the principles we discussed (intent, rhythm/pacing, hierarchy, restraint) and your peer feedback where helpful
- Both versions should demonstrate the techniques we've covered: keyframes, easing, at least one mask, and parenting somewhere. If your Version 1 doesn't include all of these, your revision is the place to add them.
- Sound is optional but considered. You can use one of the tracks I'll share, or your own music if you have something that fits. If you add sound, it should be a specific choice in service of the work. If you choose silence, that's also a real choice.
- Write a short paragraph (3-4 sentences) responding to each of the following:
    - **What did you choose to animate, and why?** Which elements of your poster get motion, and what does the motion do for them?
    - **What changed between Version 1 and Version 2?** Be specific — what did you adjust, add, or remove? Which principle drove the change?
    - **How does the motion change how the poster feels?** Compare the static poster to the animated version. What does motion add that the still didn't have?
    - **What did you decide about sound?** Did you add it, and if so why that specific choice? Or did you decide silence was right, and why?



**Grading Criteria**
- **Technical completion (40%):** Both versions submitted as MP4. Both are watchable. Techniques from prior classes applied across the two versions.
- **Revision is visible (30%):** There's a clear, identifiable difference between Version 1 and Version 2. The change is more than cosmetic — it addresses something specific in the animation, connected to one or more of the principles.
- **Reflection demonstrates understanding (30%):** Each question is answered specifically. The reflection isn't generic ("I made it better") but specific ("I delayed the subtitle by 0.5 seconds so the title could land first — that's hierarchy through timing").
